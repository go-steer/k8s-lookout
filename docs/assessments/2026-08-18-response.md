# Response to the k8sgpt assessment

The closing entry in the corpus that starts at
[2026-08-17-k8sgpt-comparison.md](2026-08-17-k8sgpt-comparison.md). It
records what we accepted and shipped, what we deferred and where it is
tracked, what we rejected and why, and the claims from the assessment
drafts that did not survive re-checking.

Scope: **k8s-lookout only.** The corpus also assessed core-agent (Q2)
and mast (Q3); those findings are those projects' to answer, and are
referenced here only where lookout had to supply something — the
non-loopback MCP bind is the one real instance.

k8sgpt was read at **`b4a86de`**. Every count in the coverage ledger
below is re-derivable from `pkg/analyzer/analyzer.go` and
`pkg/integration/*/GetAnalyzerName()` at that commit.

Tracking: epic **#291** (accepted, this round), epic **#272**
(deferred coverage).

## 1. The verdict we accepted

> We have the better architecture and the worse product.

We think that was right, and we did not argue with the four things the
corpus named as broken:

1. **No zero-arg entry point.** `k8sgpt analyze` with no flags returns
   findings. Lookout required you to already know which of thirty-odd
   commands to run.
2. **Six real detection holes** — StorageClass/PV binding, missing
   imagePullSecret, ReplicaSet `ReplicaFailure`, StatefulSet governing
   Service, IngressClass, CronJob hygiene.
3. **The contributor onramp.** No "how to add a check" doc, and behind
   the missing doc, real mechanical friction.
4. **MCP and multi-cluster ergonomics.** The whole tool surface on
   every model call, no access log, no `--context`.

All four are addressed below. What we did *not* accept is the framing
that the registry's richer `Command` contract is the onramp problem —
see §7.

## 2. What shipped

Everything here is merged and unreleased at the time of writing; it
lands in v0.22.0.

### The entry point

| | Issue | PR |
|---|---|---|
| `lookout scan` | #273 | #298 |

`lookout scan` needs nothing but a kubeconfig. Stage 1 runs every
target-free incident check into one stream under one envelope, each
finding stamped `check=<command>` — which is also the command to run
for the detail behind it. Stage 2 drills into the dependency edges of
every workload stage 1 flagged at warning or above: one cluster List
pass and N in-memory evaluations, rolled up to the outermost
controller so twenty crashlooping pods of one Deployment are one
drill-down rather than twenty. The output is the standard §4.2
envelope, so `lookout scan | lookout findings diff --store=…` works
with no glue.

A scan that could not run something says so rather than reporting a
clean bill of health it did not earn: a declined check becomes an info
`scan.check_skipped`, an erroring one a warning `scan.check_failed`
without voiding the other stages, and a timeout emits `scan.incomplete`
naming what went unrun.

Two contract tests keep it honest. `TestScanCoversEveryRegisteredCommand`
fails until every registered command is either in stage 1, in an
optional group, or in the exclusion table **with a reason string** —
so a new check cannot be added without someone deciding whether a bare
scan runs it. The reasons are the interesting artifact; they are the
first place the incident/posture line is written down per-command.

### The detection holes

| | Issue | PR | What it now catches |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state storage` | #274 | #296 | more than one default StorageClass, a PVC bound to nothing, a PV `Released`/`Failed`, a `no-provisioner` class nobody will ever satisfy |
| `state edges` ×3 | #275 | #295 | a pod naming an imagePullSecret that does not exist, a StatefulSet with no governing Service, an Ingress with no resolvable class |
| `triage delta` ReplicaSet | #276 | #292 | `ReplicaFailure` / `FailedCreate` — the quota or PodSecurity denial that creates **zero** pods, so every pod-level check we had was silent |
| `state gateway` | #277 | #297 | Gateway/GatewayClass/listener status and HTTPRoute backend resolution, discovery-gated |

The ReplicaSet one is the most valuable of the four and the corpus was
right to rank it first: it is a class of outage lookout could not
previously diagnose *at all*, because the diagnosis depends on
noticing an absence.

#276 also covered CronJob hygiene, and that half is **not** shipped:
it split into #293 and #294 because each turned out to need a decision
rather than an implementation (§6).

`state gateway` also did the structural work: it established the
discovery-gated CRD detector pattern on the read path, which was the
blocker on five deferred items (see §6). #261 closed as a result.

The schema-size numbers moved in both directions and both are worth
stating. The full surface **grew** — 31 tools / 116 KB when the
assessment measured it, 34 tools / 151 KB now, because we added
`scan`, `state storage` and `state gateway`. Breadth costs schema; the
answer is not to stop adding checks. `--profile=triage` advertises 7
tools at 51 KB, `--profile=audit` 7 at 41 KB, and `--tools=` adjusts
either one left to right. Measure yours with `lookout mcp
--list-tools`, which prints the per-tool byte cost.

Non-loopback bind is the one place a lookout change was required by
someone else's gap (GAP-12, a mast item). It stays refused by default:
a routable bind needs `--auth-token-file` **and** an explicit
`--allow-non-loopback`, compares the bearer token in constant time,
and makes the access log mandatory in that mode.

## 3. Incidents, audits, and scans — the line we drew

The corpus asked whether k8sgpt's model (one `analyze` that runs
everything) is the right one. It turns out **k8sgpt does not do that
either**, and finding that out settled the question.

`pkg/analyzer/analyzer.go` splits 14 `coreAnalyzerMap` entries from 16
`additionalAnalyzerMap` entries, and `pkg/analysis/analysis.go:395`
runs core only when no filters are set. The 16 held back are the
posture-shaped ones — Security (wildcard RBAC), NetworkPolicy, PDB,
HPA, Storage — plus ecosystem CRDs. That is almost exactly the line
`pkg/checks/audit/audit.go` already drew.

The distinction we settled on:

- An **incident** is broken now and stops being true when someone
  fixes it. It self-clears. `pod.crashloop` is gone the next scan.
- A **posture** claim is a standing property of how something is
  configured. It does not self-clear: "this Deployment has no PDB" is
  true every minute of every day until someone writes a PDB, and it
  was equally true before anything went wrong.
- A **scan** is the zero-argument reading of the first category. An
  **audit** is the deliberate reading of the second.

So `lookout scan` reports incidents, and the posture groups are one
flag away (`--include=audit`, `cloud`, `perf`, or `all`). The groups
left out are named in the summary's `skipped=` note, so they stay
discoverable while off.

Two reasons, and the second is the decisive one:

1. A healthy cluster's first scan would otherwise return hundreds of
   posture findings and nothing wrong, which teaches the reader that
   scan output is noise.
2. `findings diff` is a **transition** stream — new / ongoing /
   resolved. Posture findings never transition. Defaulting them on
   would swamp the one surface whose value is that everything in it
   changed.

This also shows up in the fingerprint recipe, which is where the two
categories were already distinguished before we had the vocabulary:
`ScanFingerprint` hashes the frozen reactive kind `k8s-event`, because
a scan sees the pull-path view of a symptom the sentinel would have
pushed. `PostureFingerprint` hashes the *detector's* kind, because
there is no event for "this workload has no PDB" and therefore nothing
to dedupe against.

### Update, 2026-08-19: both decisions made, both shipped

The two rows above are settled. They are left in the table because
this is a dated record of where the round ended, not a live tracker.

**#294 — the premise was wrong.** "This needs a cron expression
parser, i.e. a new dependency" was not true when it was written.
`robfig/cron/v3` had been a direct dependency since the `workload`
sentinel's `workload.cron_missed` landed, so
[the dependency policy](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) was never in tension
here and there was no build-versus-buy call to make. The real gap was
narrower: the sentinel could see a wedged schedule and the read path
could not. `triage delta` now reports `cron.missed` (and
`cron.unparseable`), and both paths share one parser — the new
`pkg/cronsched`, wrapping robfig, which is what kube-controller-manager
itself uses. That sharing is a correctness property rather than a
convenience: a claim that a controller missed a window is only sound
if "window" means what the controller means by it, down to the
day-of-month/day-of-week OR rule and DST.

**#293 — the answer was that the threshold is not an age.** The
question as filed ("what age threshold makes a suspended CronJob a
finding") has no good answer, because the same duration means
different things on different schedules: a week of silence has cost an
`@hourly` job 168 runs and a `@monthly` job nothing at all. `audit
workloads` therefore requires two conditions to hold together — the
suspension is older than `--cron-suspended` (default 7d), **and** at
least one activation has actually been skipped. Only the first is a
tunable; the second is structural, and it is what makes the claim
scale to the schedule instead of to the calendar.

Dating the suspension needed its own decision, since Kubernetes stores
no "suspended at" timestamp. The age is recovered from the
`managedFields` entry that owns `spec.suspend` — the same forensic
read that root-caused #320 — falling back to `lastScheduleTime` and
then to creation, with the anchor named in the finding. It is an
estimate that errs one way on purpose: that timestamp moves whenever
its manager rewrites any field it owns, so the true suspension is
always at or before what we read, and the failure mode is staying
quiet about a CronJob whose owner touches it regularly rather than
inventing one that was never suspended.

The split between the two follows §3's rule without an exception: a
missed activation clears itself the moment the schedule recovers, so
it is an incident; `suspend: true` clears only when someone edits the
spec, so it is posture.

## 7. Rejected, with reasons

Distinct from §6: these we are choosing not to do, rather than
deferring.

**In-cluster annotation/label exemptions (yardstick row 7).**
`pkg/exempt/exempt.go` rejects object annotations on purpose — "the
team requesting an exemption grants it to itself" is the failure mode,
and an exemption mechanism with no reviewer is not a control. It
rejects label selectors for a second and more decisive reason:
`findings diff` re-reads a *piped report*, with no cluster to query,
so a selector-based exemption could not be evaluated on the surface
that most needs it. This rationale is now written into
`CONTRIBUTING.md` under "two designs that look like omissions", so the
next reviewer does not read it as an oversight.

**The zone-fingerprint divergence (GAP-15) is not a defect.** A
sentinel with zone stamping hashes its zone into the fingerprint; a
read-path scan always hashes an empty zone, because a point-in-time
invocation carries no deployment identity and a guessed zone is worse
than a visible mismatch. Nothing downstream needs them to agree: the
§9.4 join keys on the *pair* `(fingerprint, resource_key)`, and the
resource key is the pin. `engine.Fingerprint` is frozen at schema v1,
and stamping a zone into `ScanFingerprint` would break the join in the
other direction — against every record from a zone-less sentinel,
which is the common deployment.

The action was a guardian test, not a code change:
`TestJoiner_ZoneMismatchStillJoins` asserts both directions join at
once, which is only possible if the resource-key pin is doing the
work. If a future change makes it fail, the join has stopped
tolerating the mismatch. There is also a user-facing note on
[Signals & fingerprints](https://go-steer.github.io/k8s-lookout/concepts/signals-and-fingerprints/).

Relatedly: `PostureFingerprint`'s empty zone is a separate, deliberate
decision (posture claims have no failure domain) and is not part of
GAP-15 at all, though the gap doc's framing blurs them.

**Registry rearchitecture.** The yardstick's row 3 comparison — a
k8sgpt analyzer is one struct and one map line, ours is a `Command`
declaration — is accurate but draws the wrong conclusion. That
declaration is why one edit produces `--help`, the MCP tool schema,
the skill reference, the site reference page, and now the kind
glossary, rather than five hand-written surfaces that drift. We added
four declared fields this round (`Kinds`, `MCPProfiles`,
`TimeoutDefault`, plus `Writer.Stamp`) and changed nothing structural.
What actually made adding a check easier was removing the accidental
friction around the registry, not the registry.

**Consuming k8sgpt's MCP server, and standalone LLM narration.** A
README section at most.

## 8. Corrections

The corpus warns that eleven claims across its drafts were wrong, in
both directions. Three more did not survive re-checking against the
tree while planning this response:

1. **The stage-2 drill-down is not an unbounded fan-out multiplier.**
   The gap doc assumed N drill-downs meant N List passes. It is one
   `state.LoadCluster` followed by N in-memory `EdgeFindings` calls —
   far cheaper than the concern assumed. It is still bounded
   (`--max-drilldown`, default 20) because unbounded output is its own
   problem, but not for the stated reason.
2. **`PostureFingerprint` was folded into GAP-15 and does not belong
   there.** Its empty zone is a different decision with a different
   justification.
3. **The zone divergence was presented as a defect** rather than as
   the documented tradeoff it already was in `docs/repo-map.md`.

And one correction to the response process rather than the corpus:
`.github/settings.yml`, added in #290, listed `build` as a required
status check. The only job by that name is the docs-site build, which
triggers on docs paths only — it would have blocked every code-only PR
the moment safe-settings was installed. Fixed in #317.

Carried forward unchanged from the summary, because it stays true: do
not claim detection breadth, do not cite k8sgpt's empty
`ADOPTERS.md`, do not call their MCP server insecure (it defaults to
stdio with no socket, which is safer than our loopback TCP; the
plaintext criticism attaches to `k8sgpt serve`'s gRPC API), do not use
their Secret handling against them without noting that our sentinel's
ClusterRole grants `secrets: list` cluster-wide by default, and do not
frame the maturity gap as "three years vs four weeks" — it is 144
contributors and 116 releases against one author, and the age framing
invites the correction.
